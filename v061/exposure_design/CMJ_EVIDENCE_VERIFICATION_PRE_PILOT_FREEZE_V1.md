## 1. Evidence-verification summary

- Direct evidence for any CMJ/lower-limb neuromuscular measure in entertainment professional wrestlers: **none identified in this search**.
- Everything below is EXT (soccer, rugby, AFL, judo, BJJ, Greco-Roman wrestling) or generic methodology.
- Decisions that the literature DOES resolve: mean-of-3 jump height remains acceptable as primary; same device/method within athlete; hands on hips; record depth, time of day and warm-up; 24 h + 48 h is the smallest useful recovery schedule.
- Decisions the literature does NOT resolve (need pilot data): inter-trial rest, immediate-post acceptable window, number of reference sessions needed, typical error, whether the secondary variable adds information in wrestlers.
- Several PMC/PubMed pages were blocked (reCAPTCHA/allowlist). Anything I could not read is marked U and not relied on.

## 3. Primary jump-height ruling

- Jump height remains PRIMARY. Session value = mean of the 3 pre-specified trials; best trial reported only as a pre-declared sensitivity descriptor, not as a second primary.
- Support: CMJ jump height shows acceptable reliability and sensitivity to match-related change in team sports (Cormack 2008a, V-abs; Claudino 2017 meta-analysis, V-abs; Silva 2018 meta-analysis, V-full: CMJ −5.5% post, −5.9% at 24 h, −3.2% at 48 h, −4.2% at 72 h in soccer, EXT; Akyildiz 2022, V-full; Marqués-Jiménez 2025, V-abs; Serrano-Huete 2023, V-abs: −12.7% by bout 5 in simulated judo contest, EXT).
- Mean vs best: I found no evidence that best-trial is superior for repeated within-athlete monitoring; multi-trial averaging reduces trial noise (Claudino 2017, Alba-Jiménez 2022, both abstract-level). Mean-of-3 therefore stays, conditional on pilot typical error. A single-trial approach (Grainger 2017 thesis, V-abs, OptoJump CV < 10%) is a practical fallback only; it is a less precise one.
- Familiarisation: learning effects are the reason for the reference-session design (Hopkins, read earlier); the number of reference sessions is pilot-determined.
- Caveat: jump height can be preserved while strategy changes (Kons judo: impulse fell after bout 3 while height did not, V-abs). Height is necessary, not sufficient, and is silent on mechanism.

## 5. Final secondary recommendation

- **At most ONE: time to take-off (contraction/movement time)**, only if the system gives force-time or equivalent timing; **zero** if a flight-time-only system is used.
- No second secondary variable is justified by verified evidence. Impulse is flagged as a pilot-assessed candidate; adding it requires reviewer approval after pilot reliability data.
- Honest strength rating: low–moderate, EXT, abstract-level for most support. This is a conservative pre-specification, not a validated marker in wrestling.

## 6. Force plate vs simple system ruling

- Jump height from impulse-momentum (force plate) and from flight time or contact mat differ; they are not interchangeable (Ruf 2024, V-full: contact mat vs impulse-momentum mean difference ~3.2 cm). Phone app and optical systems read lower than force-plate flight time by ~1 cm in the studies read (Balsalobre-Fernández 2015, V-full; Glatthorn 2011, V-abs).
- Therefore: keep same device and same method within athlete (frozen). A validated simple system is acceptable to answer the primary question.
- Lost with a simple system: time to take-off, impulse, depth, force-time shape, strategy changes. These matter only for the secondary and mechanistic questions.
- Ruling: force plate stays PREFERRED, justified only by the one conditional secondary variable plus strategy/depth information. No device selection.

## 8. Immediate-post timing evidence and recommendation

- Evidence is direction-inconsistent and timing-dependent: soccer post-match CMJ −5.5% (Silva 2018, V-full, "post" = match end, EXT); simulated judo bouts tested immediately after each bout fell to −12.7% by bout 5 (Serrano-Huete 2023, V-abs); simulated BJJ fights tested 20 min after the last fight showed a small CMJ increase, 40.8 to 42.0 cm (da Silva 2014, V-abs; post-timing 20 min taken from its Methods per the page I read); PAPE onset reported around 6–10 min and may last longer (Blazevich & Babault 2019, V-full, truncated).
- I found **no minute-scale time-course of CMJ after strenuous intermittent/contact exercise** that supports a specific window, and none in wrestling. A window would be an invented number.
- Recommendation: keep "as soon as safely/logistically feasible, exact elapsed time recorded". **No upper bound is frozen.** An upper bound must be pilot-derived (elapsed-time vs change relationship). Anything on the order of 20 min risks mixing recovery or potentiation into the signal (EXT inference).

## 9. Recovery-timepoint ruling

- 24 h: largest or near-largest deficit in several datasets (Silva 2018; Akyildiz 2022, d ≈ 1.6 for height; Marqués-Jiménez 2025 starters).
- 48 h: height still reduced in Akyildiz; peak power lower in starters (Marqués-Jiménez); judo impulse still below baseline (Orssatto 2019). 
- 72 h: deficits persisted to 72 h in Silva 2018; rugby deficits reported to ~170 h (Grainger 2017, thesis, V-abs). Keep 72 h **conditional** (practicality, athlete availability, pilot showing 48 h incomplete).
- 30–45 min: no evidence resolves it; stays PARKED.
- Ruling: **24 h + 48 h is the smallest useful schedule**, all EXT.

## 10. Fatigue / potentiation (PAPE) ruling

- PAPE/PAP literature (Blazevich & Babault 2019 V-full; Seitz & Haff 2016 V-abs; Tillin & Bishop 2009 V-abs; Rampinini 2011 V-abs) is about conditioning stimuli followed by short rest windows; applying it to a whole wrestling match is extrapolation.
- Immediate post CMJ can reflect fatigue, potentiation, or both; CMJ alone cannot separate them or central from peripheral mechanisms, nor indicate injury risk.
- Reasoned inference (not directly tested): 24 h and 48 h deficits fall beyond the minute-scale PAPE window, so they more plausibly reflect residual fatigue/damage/other. Keep **"acute net neuromuscular-performance change"**.

## 11. Combat / wrestling evidence summary (separated)

- **Professional (entertainment) wrestling:** none identified in this search.
- **Amateur wrestling:** Barbas 2011 (Greco-Roman, simulated one-day tournament, 12 wrestlers; performance declined progressively, upper body more; specific tests not identifiable from the page I read; V-abs). No lower-limb CMJ result extractable. 
- **Combat sports (EXT):** judo simulated bouts (Orssatto 2019 V-full; Kons V-abs; Serrano-Huete 2023 V-abs); BJJ simulated fights (da Silva 2014 V-abs, no impairment at 20 min). All simulated, not real competition; none involve scripted performance.
- **Team/contact (EXT):** soccer (Silva 2018, Akyildiz 2022, Marqués-Jiménez 2025), rugby (Grainger 2017), AFL (Cormack 2008b).

## 12. Professional-wrestling gap-search result

**No direct evidence identified in this search** for pre/post CMJ, neuromuscular fatigue, force-plate change, jump performance, or acute lower-extremity recovery in entertainment professional wrestlers. This is not a claim that no study exists. Limitations: general web search only, no direct PubMed/Scopus/Web of Science queries, English terms.

## 13. Updated CMJ protocol candidate (proposal)

1. Familiarisation + ≥2 rested reference sessions (number pilot-determined), same time of day, footwear, surface, warm-up, as match-day pre.
2. Match-day pre CMJ after the standard warm-up; record clock time.
3. 3 trials per session, hands on hips, self-selected depth (recorded when possible); session value = mean; inter-trial rest NOT frozen.
4. Immediate post-match CMJ as soon as safely/logistically feasible; exact elapsed time recorded; window NOT frozen. RPE after CMJ.
5. 24 h and 48 h CMJ (same method); 72 h conditional; 30–45 min PARKED.
6. Primary: mean jump height (within-athlete change). Secondary: time to take-off, only on a force-time-capable system. Covariates: depth, clock time, warm-up, soreness/readiness.
7. Same device and same calculation method throughout, per athlete.

## 14. Uncertainties requiring pilot data (not more literature)

Typical error and day-to-day variability for height (and time to take-off) in these athletes; number of reference sessions; inter-trial rest; usable immediate-post elapsed-time window; whether self-selected depth drifts pre→post and moves height; whether time to take-off adds information in wrestlers; whether 72 h is needed; behaviour of the metric under scripted, partly staged exertion; whether impulse earns a place.
