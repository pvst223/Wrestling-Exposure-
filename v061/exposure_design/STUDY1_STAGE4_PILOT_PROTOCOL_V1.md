## C. Pilot objectives (methodology only)

- **Q1** How does error of the session score depend on the number of trials averaged (1…5)? (The frozen "3" is checked, not reopened.)
- **Q2** What is the day-to-day within-person error σ_e (typical error, TE) of the session score, with uncertainty? Relative reliability as context only.
- **Q3** From which session does the systematic session (learning) effect stop?
- **Q4** How many reference sessions does the later protocol need?
- **Q5** How large is natural day-to-day variation (the noise band against which later acute changes would be read)?
- **Q6** Practicality: invalid-trial rate, time per block, adverse events, dropout.
- **Q7** Feasibility only (P2, if performed): delivery as soon as safely/logistically feasible after a match-like context; 24 h and 48 h visits.
- **Q8** If force-time-capable: reliability and non-redundancy of TTO; whether impulse merits promotion; otherwise zero secondary.
- **Q9** Given an independently locked Δ (U1), is Δ distinguishable from the day-to-day noise at the intended group level? (D1)

## E. Participants and recruitment framework

**E1. P1 target.** n = 15 healthy, trained, injury-free adults able to perform a CMJ, completing 5 sessions. **This is a pilot target with accepted imprecision.** It is NOT the Study 1 sample size, NOT a definitive reliability sample, and NOT the final Study 1 power calculation (Hopkins 2000 suggests about 50 participants for reasonable precision and notes most published reliability studies are pilots [VER]).

**E2. Eligibility and exclusion [DRAFT-OP].** Eligibility: age of legal consent; able to perform maximal jumps; no current lower-extremity pain or injury; passes the health screen in §P. **E3. Fewer than 15 complete.** The same analysis is run and the wider confidence interval is carried through the upper-limit rule in D1. No minimum-n threshold is set. If the estimate cannot be defensibly obtained, D1 is not PASS (§N).

**E4. Recruitment.** Recruitment and access do not yet exist and are an owner fact. No recruitment occurs before the conditions in §W.

## F. Session schedule

**F1. Sessions.** S1 = familiarisation. S2–S5 = reliability sessions. S1 is excluded a priori from the error estimate (§L). Sessions are 48–96 h apart, at the same clock time ± 1 h, completed within about three weeks [DRAFT-OP: the exact scheduling window is an operational detail for audit]. Deviations are recorded (§Q data fields) and are not grounds to exclude data by themselves.

**F2. Missed or incomplete sessions.** Handled by the mixed (REML) model of §K; participants with fewer than two post-familiarisation session scores contribute no information to σ_e and are reported as dropouts.

**F3. Exposure-free.** P1 sessions are exposure-free by design: no match, no standardised stressor.

**F4. P2 logistics rehearsal (if performed).** With consent and safety clearance: rehearse moving an athlete from end-of-activity to the CMJ area and delivering the scripted block; log timestamps and completion. Attempt scheduled 24 h and 48 h follow-up visits; log attendance, window deviation and burden. Immediate-post testing occurs as soon as safely/logistically feasible, with the exact elapsed time recorded and no upper bound. No 72 h visit is part of this pilot (U19 DEFER; fail-closed). No 30–45 min test point.

## G. Standardised CMJ procedure

At every session, in this order:

1. **Arrival.** Record local clock time, soreness 0–10 and readiness 0–10 (frozen covariates), prior-48 h activity, body mass, pain/injury flag. Stop rule on pain (§P).
2. **Warm-up (U4 RESOLVE).** A single written warm-up, identical in content and duration at every session, fixed before the first participant and not adjusted to data. It is low in fatigue load. Draft structure [DRAFT-OP]: light general movement, dynamic lower-limb movement, then the practice jumps in step 3. Content, duration and clock time are recorded. Any difference in warm-up between baseline and a later post condition is recorded as a known factor, not corrected.
3. **Practice jumps.** At each session start, the same script: submaximal then maximal practice jumps [DRAFT-OP: draft counts 3 submaximal, 2 maximal; the same at every session]. Fuller familiarisation instruction at S1.
4. **Recorded block.** Up to 5 recorded maximal CMJ attempts, hands on hips, self-selected countermovement depth, standard scripted instruction to jump as high as possible.
5. **Inter-trial rest.** One fixed duration, identical at every trial, session and time point (baseline and any post testing alike). **Duration: [OPEN — U5, returned for independent review; no value has been set from verified literature].** The pilot does not compare rest durations. The actual rest before each trial is recorded.
6. **Consistency.** Same device, surface, footwear standard, position marking, scripted instructions and calculation method (§I) throughout; tester recorded.
7. **Depth.** Countermovement depth is recorded where the device provides it, otherwise marked not recorded.

**Primary session score.** Y_ij = the mean jump height (cm) of the first 3 valid trials of the block for person i in session j. Trials 4–5 are recorded but diagnostic only and never enter the primary.

## H. Trial validity and replacement rules

**H1. A trial is invalid if:** hands leave hips; visible knee flexion or tuck in flight; landing off the measuring surface or non-simultaneous landing (device-dependent); a step or hop before take-off; no still start; a device or software error; or a tester-judged non-maximal attempt (with reason recorded).

**H2. Replacement.** Invalid trials are discarded and replaced, up to 2 replacements per block. All attempts and flags are retained. If fewer than 3 valid trials are obtained, the block is incomplete (a feasibility outcome). If 3 or 4 valid trials are obtained, the block is complete and the missing diagnostic trials are recorded as missing.

**H3. Re-checks.** Offline re-checks use the same written criteria, blind to the jump value, only where raw force-time data exist. No video is collected in this pilot (§P).

**H4. No extra trials.** There is no rule that adds trials to improve the estimate. The "first 3 valid trials" rule is not tuned to data.

## I. Equipment capability framework

**I1. U8a — framework (RESOLVED).** Equipment is described by capability class only; no brand or product is named or recommended.
- **Class F (force-time-capable):** records vertical ground reaction force over time, permitting a still-weighing phase, take-off detection and impulse–momentum calculation. Eligible for the conditional TTO secondary and for impulse assessment.
- **Class T (flight-time):** measures flight time (or gives jump height from it) but not force-time. Zero secondary outcomes is the valid result.
- Force plate is preferred, not required.
- Any device: calibration or zero check recorded each session; sampling rate and software version recorded; same device for an athlete throughout. A minimum sampling rate for Class F is not set because no verified source was read [NV]; the reviewer may set one.

**I2. U8b — actual pilot device/class (NOT SELECTED).** The device and class are an **owner-supplied pre-execution fact**. No device or class has been selected by this protocol or by any prior document, and none is implied. The class must be confirmed before execution (§W).

**I3. Jump-height calculation method (U7 RESOLVE).** Fixed by class before the first participant: Class F → impulse–momentum method (a trial without a valid still start is invalid and replaced, not recalculated); flight-time jump height from the same signal may be computed as a descriptor only, never as the primary. Class T → flight-time method. The same method is used for an athlete at every session and time point; values from different methods or classes are never pooled or compared; σ_e is reported per class. Basis: flight-time and impulse–momentum heights differ systematically, with flight time usually larger (about 0.5–2 cm, hands on hips, Linthorne 2001 [VER]; 3.2 cm mat versus force plate in Ruf 2024 [VER]).

## J. Outcomes

- **Primary:** session score Y_ij (§G), analysed for its day-to-day within-person error σ_e (TE) with 95% CI.
- **Supporting:** session-effect profile ŝ_j with CIs; model diagnostics; trial-count curve (Q1); best-of-3 versus mean-of-3 error (descriptive only); ICC (§K3), secondary descriptive context only.
- **Conditional secondary (Class F only):** TTO, with onset parameters **[OPEN-DEFERRED — U13: fixed before the first participant, by one documented algorithm, no manual adjustment]**. TTO earns the single conditional secondary only if (a) its error relative to its own meaningful change is acceptable under a rule set with U1, (b) onset detection is stable on every valid trial, and (c) it carries information beyond jump height. Impulse is assessed the same way and replaces TTO only if clearly superior on reliability and non-redundancy, since at most one secondary is allowed. Otherwise, and always for Class T, zero secondary is the valid result.
- **Feasibility (descriptive):** invalid-trial rate, incomplete-block rate, time per block, adverse events, attendance (§O).

## K. Reliability and statistical model

**K1. Model M1.** Y_ij = μ + a_i + s_j + e_ij; a_i ~ N(0, σ²_a) persons; s_j fixed session effects (systematic change, such as learning); e_ij ~ N(0, σ²_e). With complete balanced data, σ̂²_e is the residual mean square of the two-way ANOVA with df = (n−1)(k−1), where k is the number of post-familiarisation sessions used (4 if S2–S5 are all stable). With missing sessions, a REML mixed model is used. σ_e is a **total** day-to-day variance (true day-to-day state variation, trial noise averaged over the 3 trials, set-up and tester variation); it is not "instrument error alone".

**K2. One quantity, one name.** Typical error (TE) ≡ within-subject SD ≡ √MSE standard error of measurement ≡ σ_e, reported once, in cm, with its CI (Hopkins 2000; Weir 2005 [VER]). Equivalence fails with missing/unbalanced data (use the mixed model), with proportional error (CV% is a log-scale quantity), and with unequal error across persons (a pooled value is then an average and is labelled as such). Minimal detectable change, 1.96 × √2 × TE, is a derived transform of TE, reported descriptively only (§N).

**K3. ICC (U18 RESOLVE).** The ICC reported is Shrout–Fleiss **ICC(3,1)** (two-way mixed, single measure): ICC = (BMS − EMS)/(BMS + (k−1)·EMS), computed on post-familiarisation (post-plateau) session scores, where BMS is the between-participants mean square, EMS the residual mean square and k the number of sessions used. "Single measure" means one **session score**, which is itself the mean of the first 3 valid trials, not one jump. The formula is stated in full so that no other source's notation is needed. √EMS is TE. Rationale: a session is an ordered occasion shared by all participants, not a random calendar day; day-specific shocks belong in e_ij; systematic order/practice change is estimated through ŝ_j. Verified: Shrout & Fleiss 1979 formulas and the random/fixed distinction (full text). **Not verified and not relied on:** McGraw & Wong 1996 notation and labels; Koo & Li 2016 interpretation bands. ICC is **secondary descriptive context only**. An ICC point estimate below 0.5 (algebraically, between-person SD smaller than σ_e) may raise a **descriptive review flag** only. It is not a validated reliability threshold and cannot PASS or FAIL the protocol (U14).

**K4. Precision.** The CI for σ_e is σ̂_e·√(df/χ²_{0.975,df}) to σ̂_e·√(df/χ²_{0.025,df}), exact only under independence, equal variance and normality and approximate otherwise. For n = 15 with four stable sessions (df = 42) the 95% factors are 0.82–1.27 [DER]. Confidence factors are computed, not taken from Hopkins 2000 Table II (U22; the Table II value of 1.24 for 15 × 4 versus computed 1.22/1.27 remains unexplained).

## L. Learning / familiarisation assessment (U10)

- S1 is excluded from the error estimate a priori as familiarisation, irrespective of its data. An analysis including S1 is reported as a sensitivity analysis.
- The S2–S5 session effects ŝ_j and their confidence intervals are reported against the locked SESOI (U1) as context. **Plateau adequacy is judged jointly from the magnitude, the direction and persistence of drift, the uncertainty, and the consequences for the reliability estimate. The SESOI is not automatically the sole plateau threshold.**
- No p-value test of "no session effect" may establish plateau (a non-significant session effect does not prove absence of bias; Weir 2005 [VER]).
- σ_e and the ICC for planning come from the post-familiarisation sessions judged adequate; the all-sessions version is shown for comparison.
- If drift in S2–S5 is judged to have consequences for the reliability estimate, this is reported as a D3 MODIFY or FAIL (§N) for independent review. It does not trigger automatic extra trials.

## M. Reference-session analysis (U11)

**M1. Formula (working approximation).** Comparing one later session with the mean of k_ref reference sessions, the SD of the difference is TE × √(1 + 1/k_ref): 1.414, 1.225, 1.155, 1.118, 1.095 for k_ref = 1…5. It holds only under the prespecified assumptions: A1 independent session errors; A2 stable true state after the plateau with no systematic or person-specific drift; A3 equal error variance across persons and sessions; A4 no serial correlation.

**M2. Checks.** A1/A4: correlation of adjacent-session residuals and comparison with an AR(1) mixed model, with CIs; A2: ŝ_j profile and person-specific slope variance from a random-slope model, with CI; A3: residual spread against person mean on raw and log scale. At about four stable sessions these diagnostics have low power; absence of evidence of violation is not evidence the assumption holds, and is reported that way. An illustration on assumed models showed the formula and the chi-square CI can fail under heteroscedasticity, serial correlation or drift [CALC, V2 §C3].

**M3. Replacement if assumptions are not defensible.** Use the approved prediction-error approach in place of the formula: for each person and each target session, predict the score from the mean of the preceding post-familiarisation reference sessions and compute the RMSE of prediction errors with a person-level cluster bootstrap CI. With S2–S5, k_ref = 1 uses targets S3–S5, k_ref = 2 uses S4–S5, k_ref = 3 uses S5, so k_ref,max = 3 is the largest count the pilot can test out of sample. The formula is not forced.

**M4. Choice of reference count.** "Choose the smallest practically feasible reference count that satisfies D1 and is supported by the reference-session diagnostics/prediction-error analysis." No percentage diminishing-return rule is used. The count is a recommendation to the Study 1 design, not a Study 1 freeze.

## N. D1 PASS / MODIFY / FAIL gate

**N1. D1 is the primary measurement gate**, evaluated at the **group level for within-person change** (U20). Individual-level MDC is reported descriptively only and is not a pass/fail criterion. No individual-level classification of "change" is made or implied.

**N2. Inputs, fixed independently of pilot results:**
- Δ / SESOI (U1) — **[OPEN]**, locked by independent review, expressed in cm and as % of the athlete's own reference mean; never derived from observed TE, any pilot data, or a multiple of σ̂_e. A resource-based value (the smallest effect the study could detect) may not serve as Δ because it makes D1 pass by construction. Distribution-based conventions (Hopkins 2004, 0.2 × between-athlete SD) are contextual benchmarks only.
- Feasible Study 1 range [N_min, N_max] (U21) — **[OPEN]**, owner-declared and reviewer-accepted.
- Eligible k_ref values: 1 … k_ref,max (3 under the §F design).
- α = 0.05 and power = 0.80, as the illustrative planning convention.

**N3. Calculation.** σ̂_e^U = upper limit of the two-sided 95% CI for σ_e on the post-familiarisation sessions judged adequate (§K4). σ_Δ^U = σ̂_e^U·√(1 + 1/k_ref), or the prediction-error RMSE where the §M2 checks do not support the formula. N_req(k_ref) = the smallest N giving at least 0.80 power by the non-central t (two-sided α = 0.05) for the standardised effect Δ/σ_Δ^U.

**N4. Terminology.** N_req is **an illustrative measurement-error-limited Study 1 feasibility calculation, not the final Study 1 power analysis or frozen Study 1 sample size.** It does **not** incorporate all future Study 1 variance components — including true between-person response heterogeneity — and therefore cannot substitute for the eventual Study 1 sample-size analysis. Real heterogeneity can only raise the required N, so N_req is a lower bound. The following detectability table is illustrative only and says nothing about what Δ or N should be (k_ref = 3, multiplier 1.155):

| σ_e / Δ | R = σ_Δ/Δ | N (z-approx.) | N (exact t) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.58 | 2.6 | 5 |
| 1.0 | 1.15 | 10.5 | 13 |
| 1.5 | 1.73 | 23.5 | 26 |
| 2.0 | 2.31 | 41.9 | 44 |
| 3.0 | 3.46 | 94.2 | 97 |

**N5. Verdict:**
- **PASS** if some eligible k_ref gives N_req ≤ N_min.
- **MODIFY** if N_req exceeds N_min but not N_max for the best eligible k_ref (Study 1 would need the larger declared size), or if a permitted procedural change that does not touch the primary outcome (a reviewed rest value, script, tester training, device class, set-up) is expected to reduce σ_e. A MODIFY is reported with what changed and followed by a re-estimate, not an automatic repeat of the pilot.
- **FAIL** if N_req > N_max for every eligible k_ref.
- **Fail-closed:** if σ̂_e^U cannot be defensibly obtained (insufficient adequate sessions, failed diagnostics with no usable replacement), D1 is **not PASS**.

**N6. What MODIFY may NOT include.** "More trials" is **not** a MODIFY pathway. The primary remains the mean of the first 3 valid trials; trials 4–5 remain diagnostic only; no tuning of the frozen primary. Any proposal to change the 3-trial primary requires separate independent review and is not a D1 outcome. No threshold may be invented after results are seen.

**N7. Wording (carry exactly).** "D1 compares the upper 95% confidence limit of the day-to-day within-person error, scaled by the reference-session multiplier (or replaced by prediction error where the assumptions are not defensible), with the independently locked SESOI at the owner-declared range of feasible Study 1 sizes. D1 is evaluated at the group level. It is an illustrative measurement-error-limited Study 1 feasibility judgement, not the final Study 1 power analysis or a frozen Study 1 sample size, and it is not evidence about wrestlers."

**N8. Overall decision.** Following the V2 framework (criteria D1–D11, tabled in §O for D2–D11): **PASS** = D1–D4, D7–D9 (as applicable) and D11 pass, and D5, D6, D10 are non-FAIL; the measurement protocol becomes a freeze candidate subject to independent audit. **MODIFY** = no FAIL and at least one MODIFY: apply only permitted modifications, repeat the minimum necessary sessions **once**, return for independent review. **FAIL** = a FAIL on D1, D2, D4, D7 or D11: do not proceed as designed; independent review decides among restructuring, more evidence, or reconsidering the measurement. Failure does not automatically reopen Candidate #2. "One modification cycle" is a project governance rule that triggers mandatory independent review; it is not a statistical or biological law.

## P. Ethics, safety and data governance (U9 — requirements only)

**P1. Sequencing.** No participant is recruited and no data are collected until the responsible ethics review body has issued a documented determination (approval or documented exemption). **No ethics determination currently exists, and none is claimed.** Which body applies, and whether the pilot is exempt, are governance events outside this protocol.

**P2. Consent.** Written informed consent covering: purpose (a methods pilot, not a wrestling hypothesis test), procedures, maximal-effort jumping, time, risks, voluntariness and withdrawal without penalty, data handling, pseudonymous IDs, and (for P2) that participation has no bearing on employment or standing with any promotion.

**P3. Screening.** Health and injury screening before each session. Pain or injury at arrival is recorded and stops maximal jumping.

**P4. Stop rules.** Stop on pain, dizziness, instability, or participant request. Every adverse event is logged and handled by the adverse-event procedure below. For P2 immediate-post testing, the tester or medical staff may stop the session at their discretion.

**P5. Adverse-event handling [DRAFT-OP].** Stop testing; provide first aid or summon the responsible clinician; record event, time, action and outcome; report to the ethics body per its requirements; review screening and stop rules after any event (D11).

**P6. Oversight.** A named responsible clinician or qualified first-aid person is present for any immediate-post P2 testing. **The name is an owner-supplied fact [OPEN field].**

**P7. Data protection.** Pseudonymous IDs only; no names, faces, video or footage; **no footage or video collection is part of this pilot protocol**; no identifiable data in the repository. Footage rights remain UNRESOLVED.

## Q. P2-absent safeguards (U23 — REVIEW)

If P2 is not performed, the following uncertainty remains: whether σ_e from healthy adults applies to wrestlers (body mass, motivation, pain, schedule, baseline drift), whether immediate-post delivery at a venue is safe and achievable, acceptability, and follow-up attendance. The proposed safeguards, for **independent acceptance or rejection (U23)**:

1. Wrestler-specific σ_e is estimated from Study 1's own exposure-free reference sessions and analysed without reference to post-match outcomes.
2. Follow-up attendance is monitored descriptively in Study 1.
3. Immediate-post deliverability and safety cannot be learned without trying; either the reviewer accepts a stated safety plan, or Study 1 includes a pre-specified run-in with predefined stopping rules. Whether (3) is acceptable is a reviewer decision.

The designer does not decide this. Until the reviewer accepts these safeguards, the P2-absent path cannot be frozen.
