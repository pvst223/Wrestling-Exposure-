## 1. Stage-D postmortem
**What failed.** One deterministic feature — "≥ 2 person boxes in most sampled frames" — cannot separate live action from other broadcast material that also contains several people. On CHECK it called 29 NON_MATCH shots (99.7 s: stage/crowd/promo scenes) and 2 REPLAY shots (35.1 s) LIVE. P1 48.04% > 20% → STOP. This is a failure of that approach, not of identity, anatomy or exposure in general (none of those were inputs).
**Why it was predictable in hindsight (recorded, not used to rescue).** (i) A person count carries no scene semantics. (ii) BUILD contained no REPLAY and no NON_MATCH inside the match, so BUILD could not reveal the failure mode; the reviewer had already recorded this as a major limitation. (iii) The all-LIVE comparator (52.15% on CHECK) shows the rule had essentially no discriminating power over a constant.
**Post-hoc descriptive observation (NOT authorized for any rule development).** In the CHECK reference labels, the first 160.9 s (451.5–612.4 s) are 95.1% LIVE_ACTION (152.967 s) with the rest a single PIP-ambiguous shot (7.933 s); from 612.4 s to the end of the source (166.7 s) the owner labelled NON_MATCH / REPLAY / one CLOSE_UP. BUILD's match segment (151–449 s) is 83% LIVE_ACTION and 17% PIP-UNKNOWN. So in this one condensed edit, almost all of the live/not-live contrast was **before vs after the owner-labelled match action** (match start/end were NO_D, so "match window" is inferred, not marked), and within the match window there was little non-live content to classify. Consequence: a scene classifier, even a good one, would mostly re-derive "when did the match start and end", i.e. D. This is one match from one source and is a hypothesis, not a finding.
**Real design lesson.** The target X2 (observable live time) was chosen because it was computable, then a classifier was built for it. Nobody had shown that X2 is biologically relevant to lower-extremity fatigue or informative beyond D (P5 was never reachable at Stage D). The next step should start from the biological construct, not from what the pipeline can compute.

## 2. What the biology needs
Question: does match exposure cause acute lower-extremity neuromuscular deterioration (CMJ pre/post)? Plausible drivers: time under load (duration), repeated eccentric/impact loading (landings, jumps, bumps/falls, running, sprinting-type efforts), overall effort/intensity, and wrestler role (who takes the bumps). Broadcast video measures none of these reliably at present. Any exposure variable must (a) be wrestler-specific or justify why it is shared, (b) have an argued mechanism to lower-extremity loading, (c) be measurable without unvalidated components or with their validation shown, (d) show incremental information over D, which is the cheapest comparator.

## 4. Recommended exposure strategy
1. **D is currently the strongest defensible exposure comparator** and stays the mandatory baseline (always reported, bell-to-bell, from an event timing log in the prospective study rather than from edited video).
2. **Do not pursue a scene/time classifier (B) as an exposure route.** At most, boundary detection of the match window can serve to *measure D* on retrospective footage.
3. **For the CMJ study, wrestler-specific exposure should come first from non-CV measures** (F1 sRPE and, if feasible, F2 wearable HR/IMU), recorded prospectively with D. These are simpler, validated elsewhere, and avoid dependence on unvalidated CV. They are the comparator any CV-derived exposure must beat.
4. **CV-derived, wrestler-specific exposure (C/D/E) remains exploratory**: it is justified only if (a) a loading-relevant, human-codable event construct exists and is observable (next experiment), (b) identity/ownership attribution on such events is shown with abstention on fresh data, and (c) it adds information beyond D and F1/F2.

## 5. Exact role, if any, for preserved identity/anatomy
None now. Their only legitimate future role is **attributing human-defined discrete events to wrestler A or B (or abstaining)** — "who took this bump/landing" — where identity supplies who and ownership supplies whose landmarks, never the reverse. They would **not** supply COM, distance, speed, acceleration, impact load or pose-derived intensity (each needs its own construct/measurement justification and calibrated camera geometry). Anatomy V1 stays frozen; its contact/overlap limitation and the C3/Tier-A kills stand. Before any use: event-level attribution accuracy with abstention on development data, then fresh data, with a pre-registered criterion; this is a separate future experiment, not this one.

## 6. Broadcast vs fixed camera
Stage D strengthens, but does not by itself decide, the case for fixed cameras. Its failure was about scene semantics of edited video (non-match material, replays, condensed edits, NO_D) — problems a continuous fixed camera removes by construction, and the postmortem suggests scene classification mostly re-derives D. Combined with known broadcast problems (cuts, zoom, occlusion in contact), the sensible two-regime plan is: **broadcast = retrospective development only; the eventual prospective wrestler-testing study should plan for fixed/calibratable camera(s) plus non-CV measures (D, sRPE, wearables)**, subject to access and rights. This stage does not authorize acquisition.

## 7. ONE proposed next experiment: Exposure-Construct Observability and Reliability Audit (human-coded; no classifier)
**Scientific question.** Can a small, pre-specified set of discrete, lower-extremity-relevant exposure events be (a) recognised and (b) coded reliably by a human from edited broadcast footage, and what fraction of recognised events is actually observable on screen? This decides whether any video-derived, wrestler-specific exposure construct is worth engineering, before any CV is built.
**Measurement candidate.** Within the match window of existing development footage, the owner codes per event: time (0.1 s), class ∈ {BUMP (body-to-mat/floor impact), LANDING (landing on feet from a jump, leap, springboard or dismount)}, **recipient wrestler** (A / B / UNCLEAR), and observability ∈ {FULL, PARTIAL (cut away/obstructed during the event), INFERRED (occurred but not shown)}. D is also recorded for the same window. No other classes; no intensity grading.
**Why it follows from Stage D.** The failure showed we built a classifier for a construct never shown to matter. This experiment tests the construct's *observability* with the cheapest instrument (a human), before spending on CV.
**Reused frozen components.** Existing development footage and frozen shot boundaries; the labelling-tool architecture (hash-chained, append-only, clerical-corrections-only) extended only by an event-coding form (a new, separately audited tool); frozen protocol discipline. **Forbidden:** Rule V1/V1.1, any classifier, identity/anatomy/ownership/masks/COM/distance/pose outputs, Holdout #2, Gemini, fresh acquisition, relabelling BUILD/CHECK shot labels, any use of Stage-D CHECK data to tune anything.
**Development data allowed.** The four burned clips and the full Reigns–Bálor source (all 779 s, declared development; Stage D consumed it as a classifier test set but this is a different construct and no rule is tuned on it). Holdout #2 stays sealed; **no fresh data** in this experiment — if it passes, a fresh-match stage would require a separate ruling.
**Ground truth.** Owner coding (non-independent), then a blind **intra-rater re-code of 100% of the material ≥ 7 days later** (footage is short), analysed by event matching (±0.5 s, same class). Second rater if one becomes available (R4 stays unmet otherwise). Coding protocol and category definitions written for audit before coding; no CV information visible.
**Prospective criteria (reviewer-set utility choices, not statistically derived; tiny sample).**
- **PASS (construct worth carrying forward):** observable fraction (FULL / all recognised events) ≥ 70%, intra-rater event agreement (matched events / union) ≥ 0.80, recipient identifiable (not UNCLEAR) in ≥ 80% of FULL events.
- **MODIFY:** any one criterion within 10 points below its bound, or a single class (BUMP or LANDING) passes alone → refine the definition once.
- **KILL (abandon discrete video-observable events as an exposure candidate):** observable fraction < 50%, or agreement < 0.65, or recipient identifiable < 60% of FULL events, or fewer than 20 recognised events in the available footage (insufficient to judge → report INCONCLUSIVE rather than pass).
**What would make us abandon the candidate.** Any KILL condition. Then the exposure plan is D plus non-CV wrestler-specific measures (sRPE, wearables) and a fixed-camera study design without CV-derived exposure.
**Is an MP4 useful?** Not for this experiment (it is a human-coding reliability audit). A private overlay of coded events on footage may be offered only after results are frozen and only if the reviewer asks.
**What it does not establish.** Biological relevance of BUMP/LANDING counts to CMJ change (that needs the prospective study), identity or anatomy performance, or any workload validity.

## 9. Evidence required before exposure may be paired with CMJ outcomes
1. A pre-specified exposure variable with a stated mechanism to lower-extremity loading.
2. Measurement validity: reliability (intra-/inter-rater or test-retest), observability, and for any CV-derived variable, accuracy against human ground truth on **fresh** data with pre-registered pass/kill criteria and fail-closed abstention.
3. Wrestler-specificity: for CV-derived exposure, event-level identity attribution validated on fresh data; otherwise use measures that are wrestler-specific by collection (sRPE, wearables).
4. Incremental information over D (and over sRPE/wearables if collected), assessed on the eventual sample, with the number of matches per wrestler justified for the CMJ analysis.
5. Camera regime appropriate to the claim (fixed/calibrated for anything physical; broadcast only for coarse retrospective use).
6. Separate reviewer ruling for each step; no COM, distance, speed, acceleration, impact-load or pose-intensity metric without its own construct and calibration evidence.
Until all of these hold, the only defensible exposure for the CMJ study is D (plus prospectively collected non-CV measures), reported with its limits.
