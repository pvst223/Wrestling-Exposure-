## 2. Evidence base

YouTube's help page (earlier read): CC BY is only for content the uploader can license, and YouTube "can't grant you rights to use someone else's content."
- Library of Congress Copyright Office: works published or registered in the US in 1930 entered the public domain on January 1, 2026; "even when copyright protection ends ... it may still be subject to other protections"; renewal rules after that are complicated.
- Wikimedia Commons "Videos of professional wrestling" category: 12 files, mostly 1-3 minute historical clips (1930s-1951) plus one 33-minute 2021 match and a GIF; the category page states no license (per-file pages would have to be checked).
- Chicago Film Archives (Russ and Sylvia Davis Collection, Series I): 208 wrestling matches from the 1950s, dates uncertain; the collection page does not name the rights holder; the archive's footage policy says it holds copyright to roughly 70% of its footage, the copyright status of some footage is unknown, and licensing/access is governed by a paid agreement with a request form.

## 4. Can any surviving route supply the frozen Holdout #2 requirements?
- **Route 2 (researcher-created)** is the only one that can plausibly meet Q1 + Q2 for every depicted person without anyone's permission except people the owner recruits directly, and it can be designed to meet every minimum without weakening it: at least 4 clips; at least 8 named identities (pseudonymous IDs, none from the development list); at least 150 units (60 target, 60 negative, referee and staff/spectator strata at 15 or more) by recruiting performers, referees, staff and attendees who sign releases; at least 2 distinct events and 2 sources (distinct sessions/dates and distinct camera setups, subject to the reviewer ruling that such sessions count as events/sources); each target in at least 4 distinct shots via multi-camera cutting. All of it fixed in a written production protocol before any filming and before any model run; nothing chosen by expected model performance. Costs and risks (not legal conclusions): the population differs from professional broadcast footage, so results describe that population only; staged grappling carries injury risk and probably needs an ethics/safety review, insurance, and exclusion of minors; recruited performers must not be under contracts that assign their image rights elsewhere (a factual question each performer answers in the release); the reviewer must accept that a researcher-produced sample is a valid Holdout #2 population for a method whose stated purpose is entertainment-wrestling footage.
- **Routes 3-5** cannot realistically supply all minimums alone: the public-domain supply is very small and old (inference), the archival collection is one source and its rights are unverified, and open-licensed full events are unproven. They could supplement route 2 only after UR items are resolved.
- **Domain shift:** old black-and-white archival footage or amateur staged footage would differ greatly from the development data. That is not a performance-based selection, but it changes what a Holdout result can be said to describe, and it must be disclosed and approved prospectively.
- Conclusion: no no-outreach route is currently PASS; route 2 is the only one that can realistically satisfy Q1 + Q2 and the frozen requirements, conditional on the reviewer accepting its population and on an ethics/safety plan.
