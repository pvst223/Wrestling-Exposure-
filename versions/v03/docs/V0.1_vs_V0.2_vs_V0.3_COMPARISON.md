**Everything here is in-sample development performance.** The clip informed the design of V0.2 and V0.3, the benchmark is single-reviewer and non-blinded, and there is no ground truth for landmarks, COM or identity. These numbers say nothing about generalisation. Source: `out/comparison_metrics_v03.json` (produced by `src/evaluate_v01_v02_v03.py`, which asserts the frozen benchmark hash e27aa114…) and the visual adjudication in `docs/adjudication_verdicts_v03.json`. ## 3. Recovery of V0.2 rejections, and what that cost
Of V0.1's 507 tracked frames that are outside every known/inferred-bad set (and not seeds): V0.2 kept 293 and dropped 198; **V0.3 keeps 359 and drops 143.** V0.3 emits 72 of V0.2's 198 dropped frames; it drops 12 that V0.2 kept. Net emissions vs V0.2: +94 / −17 (+77). Of the 94 new emissions, 16 are inside the known/inferred-bad set; the V0.2→V0.3 difference inside that set is +16 / −5.
**Where the recovery comes from:** of V0.3's 94 new emissions, 85 were V0.2 `OVERLAP_UNVERIFIED` frames (15 of them inside the known/inferred-bad set), 6 were V0.2 PREDICTED frames now measured, and only **1** was a V0.2 `IDENTITY_NOT_MATCHED` frame (plus 1 `IDENTITY_AMBIGUOUS`, 1 `PROBATION`). The relaxation of the overlap rule produced essentially all of the recovery; the multi-cue identity produced almost none (§5).

## 10. Coverage vs silent-error risk (summary)
On this clip V0.3 emits **+77** more A frames than V0.2 (330 → 407). The recovered frames outside the known-bad set looked sound in a 14-frame sample (0 contaminated, CI up to 22%). But the same relaxation re-admits skeletons inside the known/inferred-contaminated ranges (33 → 44 emitted; sampled: 0 clean of 18), the "VERIFIED" tier does not separate them from sound ones, and B shows wrong-person emissions. V0.3 should therefore be read as **more coverage at higher residual contamination risk than V0.2**, not as a strict improvement.
