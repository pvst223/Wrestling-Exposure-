# V0.3 developer report

**Frozen.** V0.3 was run once on the 1,501-frame development clip and not modified afterwards (hashes verified). V0.1 and V0.2 were not touched. All results are **in-sample development results on the same clip that informed V0.2 and V0.3**; they do not demonstrate generalisation, and nothing here validates identity, landmarks or the COM against ground truth. Numbers: `V0.1_vs_V0.2_vs_V0.3_COMPARISON.md`. Design: `V0.3_ARCHITECTURE_AND_CHANGELOG.md`.

## 1. Bottom line
1. V0.3 emits **407** A frames vs V0.2's 330 (V0.1: 752), still with **0 off-image predictions** and **0 false camera cuts** (identical to V0.2; in-sample). In none of the 18 labelled wrong-person frames does it emit A on the box V0.1 wrongly selected.
2. The extra coverage (+94 / −17) comes almost entirely from **relaxing the V0.2 overlap rule** (85 of 94 new emissions were V0.2 `OVERLAP_UNVERIFIED`). **The multi-cue identity recovered 1 frame that V0.2's identity gate had rejected.** The stated goal "do not require the target's thighs to be visible" is **not met in practice**: 636 frames are `IDENTITY_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` (e.g., the clean Cena close-ups I viewed at f692, f725, f732, f736).
3. **Contamination was not reduced.** The same 4 of 16 viewed contaminated frames are emitted as in V0.2 (270, 275, 950, 1075), and 16 more frames inside the known/inferred-contaminated ranges are newly emitted (33 → 44 in total). In my sample of 18 emissions in that set, 8 were contaminated/wrong-person, 9 unsure, 0 clearly clean. The new "VERIFIED" tier does not separate these from sound frames.
4. Recovered frames **outside** the known-bad set looked sound in a small sample (0/14 contaminated, 12/14 plausible, 2 unsure; CI up to 22% contaminated).
5. Tracking B introduced a new error surface: 3 of 10 sampled B emissions were on the wrong body (Cena, f1166/f1179/f1204), and at f1114 A and B both emit COMs 12 px apart.
6. **Net reading:** more coverage at a higher residual contamination risk than V0.2, on this clip. I would not describe V0.3 as an improvement in single-body integrity.

## 3. What could not be implemented reliably, and what I did instead (not silently substituted)
* **Learned re-identification embedding:** no pretrained re-ID weights could be obtained (the model hub is blocked from this environment). Substitute: interpretable colour histograms. Consequence: weak appearance evidence.
* **Per-person segmentation:** unavailable. Substitute: polygons from the pose keypoints, which are imprecise in bent/lying poses (visible in the seed sheet). Consequence: region histograms mix body, background and the other wrestler.
* **Online appearance-model updating:** deliberately not done (self-reinforcing capture risk with no ground truth to catch it). Consequence: models are static; a lighting change lowers support rather than being adapted to.
* **Calibrated probabilities:** impossible without ground truth. Substitute: ordinal scores, documented as provisional.
* **Proof that a skeleton is one body:** not available from 2D keypoints; only plausibility tests.
* **Independent labels:** none exist; all adjudication is mine (single reviewer, non-blinded to the overlay text).

## 4. Known-failure analysis (by mechanism, with evidence frames)
1. **Persistent contamination passes as "VERIFIED".** Temporal coherence compares a skeleton with the identity's own previous skeleton, so a merged skeleton that persists is coherent with itself. All 387 VERIFIED emissions rest on this; the appearance check never fired positively because only the two thighs (mass 0.20) separate the wrestlers and the rule requires 0.25 (a design inconsistency found only after the run; not fixed, per the freeze). Evidence: f269–279 (referee striped shirt merged with Cena's denim legs, MEASURED), f934–957 (Cena carrying B), f1073–1093 and f1114 (ground pile).
2. **B assigned to Cena.** f1166, f1179, f1204: B's candidate box contains both bodies in a ground pile; B's cues (continuity from the previous B box, weak appearance) pass the margins. f1114: both A and B emit with almost the same COM.
3. **Identity evidence collapses in close-ups / after cuts.** 636 `IDENTITY_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` frames; in the 4 sampled V0.1-tracked frames of this type, Cena was alone and clearly visible (f692, 725, 732, 736).
4. **Unnecessary overlap rejection from unrelated detections.** f827: a crowd detection overlapping A's box triggers overlap context, and with no positive coherence evidence yet A is rejected; f820 similar.
5. **Static, thin appearance models.** 4 views per wrestler, one referee-type exemplar; noise estimate from few pairs; unvalidated for any other person or lighting.
6. **Pose-model limits (stage 1, unchanged):** merged detections give a single hybrid skeleton with no neighbour signal; keypoints for lying/bent bodies are poor.
7. **Referee-merge (f245–279):** the NEITHER class cannot prevent a *skeleton* from mixing a referee's torso with Cena's legs when the detector returns one box.

## 5. New failure modes introduced relative to V0.2
(a) re-admission of contaminated skeletons via the overlap relaxation (+16 / −5 inside the known/inferred-bad set; sample: 1 clearly contaminated, 6 unsure of the 7 viewed); (b) wrong-person emissions for the second tracked wrestler; (c) coincident A/B COMs; (d) a "VERIFIED"/MEASURED label that overstates what was checked (f270, f275, f1075 are MEASURED/VERIFIED); (e) dependence on manual seeds whose polygons are imprecise; (f) increased complexity (115 config constants, none validated).

## 6. Unresolved-reason breakdown
See comparison doc §5. In short: 636 insufficient identity evidence, 104 contra-cue (mostly correct: the best candidate is the other person), 96 no candidate, 70 low score, 62 anatomy fail (48 shared-joint conflict), 54 overlap-unverified, 19 merge-risk-unverified, 17 probation, 11 ambiguity. Rough sampled judgement (n = 8, very uncertain): overlap/anatomy/probation rejections were justified, insufficient-evidence rejections were not.

## 7. Integrity statements
* No frame-specific exceptions in code. Frame numbers appear only in `seeds_v03_manual.json`.
* Development contact with the clip: a smoke test on frames 0–199 (it displayed cue values at frames 99, 100, 150, 175; 100 and 175 are benchmark frames), two pre-run design iterations (documented in the changelog), a seed-verification sheet. The benchmark metrics were first computed **after** the single full run. After results were seen, nothing was changed.
* The benchmark and the adjudication samples are in-sample and single-reviewer. The benchmark's seed frames were excluded; the viewed samples were drawn with a fixed seed after the run.
* V0.2's `OVERLAP_UNVERIFIED` rule was a deliberate fail-closed choice; V0.3 relaxes it as the specification required. The result is a measurable shift in the coverage/risk trade-off, reported above.

## 8. Not claimed
That V0.3 identifies the wrestlers correctly in general; that any skeleton is single-body; that any COM approximates true COM; that confidence/tier values are probabilities; that the 20/0 cut result generalises; that V0.3 generalises to unseen footage.

## 9. Options for a later version (not started; nothing was tuned in response to these results)
Independent frame-level labels (identity and contamination) from someone who has not seen V0.2/V0.3 outputs; a per-person mask or instance model so that two touching bodies are separated before pose estimation; a re-ID model with weights that can be obtained and validated; requiring *appearance-based* positive evidence for overlap/merge cases (or treating temporal coherence as insufficient on its own); an exclusivity rule between the two wrestlers' skeletons; a clean holdout clip evaluated once with pre-registered metrics.
